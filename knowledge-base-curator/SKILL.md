# Knowledge Base Curator

Keep project knowledge trustworthy, discoverable, attributable, and recoverable. “Current” is a controlled status supported by authority and evidence, not the newest filename or edit time.

## Build a Document Responsibility Map

Identify the project's authoritative sources and give each knowledge class a single job, for example:

- scope, requirements, and approved baselines;
- schedules, budgets, contracts, designs, or specifications;
- operating and safety procedures;
- active tasks, decisions, risks, and changes;
- acceptance evidence and status reporting;
- historical, legal, or audit records.

For each class, record the owner, approver, canonical location, status vocabulary, revision rule, effective scope, retention rule, and recovery path.

## Reconcile the Facts

1. Define scope by project, workstream, site or system, document class, and cutoff date.
2. Inventory each item with identifier, revision, status, owner, approver, effective date, and supersession link.
3. Establish precedence rules from project governance; do not infer authority from storage location or recency.
4. Verify current-state claims against primary evidence and accountable approval.
5. Classify each item:
   - current fact;
   - active draft, plan, risk, or pending decision;
   - durable decision rationale;
   - superseded duplicate or obsolete draft;
   - conflicting or unverified record.
6. Reconcile dependencies: an approved change must appear consistently in every schedule, budget, design, procedure, risk, system, or stakeholder record it affects.
7. Route unresolved conflicts to the accountable authority rather than silently selecting a winner.

Do not mark planned work complete because an artifact exists; require the acceptance evidence promised by the plan.

## Clean Safely

- Replace uncontrolled duplicates with references to the canonical record.
- Archive superseded material with revision, reason, owner, retention class, and recovery path.
- Permanently delete only when project governance, contractual duties, regulation, legal hold, and audit requirements allow it.
- Retain durable decisions, approvals, incident evidence, and rationale needed to explain current constraints.
- Update inbound references when records move, change authority, or are retired.
- Record blockers, conflicts, and unverified claims honestly.

## Validate

Validate links, permissions, metadata, revision chains, effective dates, supersession, retention, contradictory status, and whether intended users can reach the authoritative record. Use domain-specific checks needed to substantiate key claims.

Review the final change set for duplicated ownership, orphaned references, stale dates, scope confusion, and aspirations presented as completed facts.

## Handoff

Summarize which sources now own which facts, what moved or was archived, how it can be recovered, what evidence supports current status, and which conflicts, approvals, risks, or actions remain open.
